# App Store ASO pass and independent review, 2026-10-28
## Review outcome

The proposed metadata in commit `1691296` was independently reviewed against pre-run snapshot `654d180`. The proposed title, subtitle and keyword removals were restored, except exact Japanese and Chinese display-word duplicates, with two independently evidenced Japanese terms added. Prior copy improvements are retained, with translation and consistency corrections in German, Dutch, French, Japanese, Russian and Traditional Chinese. The nine opening lines no longer say the estimate, target, trend and one-year history appear in one view: the history is a separate screen in the current app. This is a conservative correction, not proof that every restored term is optimal. Checks ran on October 28 and resumed October 29 after an overnight interruption. Positions are proxies for the live listing and can change. Neither titles nor keywords in the repository have been uploaded.

The original report claimed that byte reductions justified removal and that every extra US field contributed distinct complete queries. Neither assertion is established. Apple’s [platform reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/platform-version-information/) says 100 bytes, while its [product page guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/) says 100 characters. The brief requires preserving a live field solely over bytes; all overages are listed below. The [localization table](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-store-localizations/) lists the nine additional US languages, but does not itself explain search indexing or cross-field word combinations. The distinct-query goal remains unproven and the restored extra fields overlap. For example, `healthkit` occurs in all nine, `cardiofitness` in seven, and `training` in four. These existing fields do not meet the distinct-group goal. No speculative restructuring was made while removal combinations remain unverified.

The original report overstated review completeness: an absent one-word SERP does not establish that a removed word lacks useful combinations. Autocomplete is demand evidence, not ranking evidence, and an app suggestion is not a bare query. Checks below are the actual responses from this independent run, including zero suggestions. They do not establish search volume.
